Getting Started

Ampz is a professional-grade guitar amp modeling app for iOS, powered by Neural Amp Modeling (NAM) technology. NAM uses deep neural networks (WaveNet and LSTM architectures) trained on real amplifier recordings to faithfully reproduce the sonic characteristics of physical guitar amplifiers.

Ampz targets ultra-low latency: using Apple's RemoteIO Audio Unit with 64-sample buffers at 48 kHz, it achieves buffer latency as low as 1.33 ms. Requirements: iOS/iPadOS 18.0 or later, and a USB or Lightning audio interface with an instrument-level input for the best latency and signal quality. iPad uses an adaptive sidebar layout with scaled controls.

Quick start: connect your guitar via a USB audio interface, open Ampz (the NAM Amplifier slot is ready by default), download a model from the Tone3000 tab or import a .nam file via My Models, then play. Add effects by double-tapping empty slots in the chain, and use the preset chevrons or the preset name/number display to browse the 100 preset slots.

NAM Amplifier

Neural Amp Modeling captures a real guitar amplifier's sonic behavior with a neural network trained on measurements taken at thousands of signal levels and frequencies. Ampz runs the NAM inference engine natively in C++ using the NeuralAudio library with Eigen linear algebra and Apple's Accelerate framework for SIMD optimization — authentic amp tone at under 2.5 ms latency.

Ampz doesn't ship with bundled models — you bring your own, either from the in-app Tone3000 browser or by importing a .nam file from your device. Any of the 16 effect-chain slots can host its own NAM model, not just the default Amplifier slot, so you can run several amp captures at once — for example a clean amp feeding a boosted lead amp.

NAM knobs:

The algorithm bar above the knobs cycles the output mode — Raw (untouched model output), Normalized (levels every model to consistent loudness, the default), or Calibrated (matches real-world gain staging, adding an Input Cal knob and Cal In toggle) — plus Gate, EQ, and Bypass toggles for the slot.

Effects Chain

A full pedalboard on your phone: 16 drag-to-reorder slots, 17 effect types across 10 categories, all running natively on iOS with studio-grade DSP. Tap a slot to select it, double-tap an empty slot to open the Effect Browser, flick up or down to bypass, or long-press and drag to reorder. The IN tab opens the Chain Overview and the OUT tab shows whether the rig is feeding mono or stereo. A DSP badge under the toolbar opens a per-pedal cost panel with a benchmark of every NAM model in your library.

Delay, tremolo, and chorus support tempo sync to the global BPM (quarter, eighth, dotted-eighth, sixteenth, and triplet subdivisions). Reverb and delay support spillover ("Effect Trails" in Settings) so tails decay naturally through a bypass or preset switch instead of cutting off abruptly.

Signal Path: Split Lanes and the Mix Block

A rig does not have to be one straight line. Ampz can split the chain once into two parallel lanes, A and B, and merge them again through a grey Mix pedal. Pedals before the split feed both lanes; pedals after the merge run on the sum. That covers a dual-amp rig, a wet/dry rig, or a dual mono rig with lane A in the left speaker and lane B in the right.

Tap IN for the Chain Overview and its Signal Path row: Straight (one row of 16), Middle Split (4, then 4 + 4, then 4), Immediate Split (6 + 6 from the input, then 4) and Dual Mono (Immediate Split with A left and B right). A template never deletes or reorders pedals; it moves the split and merge points and fills the new regions with empty slots. The template the rig is in is highlighted, and Straight remembers the lanes so a split template puts the same pedals back. You can also split by hand: select a pedal and tap Split, drop a dragged pedal onto another to run them in parallel, or drag the Split node and the Mix pedal onto a pedal to move the cut points.

Mix controlRangeWhat it does

Lane tags keep the picture honest: a lane the crossfade has silenced dims, L and R mark a dual mono rig, B Ø marks an inverted lane, and DRY marks an empty lane that passes your signal through unchanged beside a lane with pedals.

Preset System

Ampz has 100 preset slots (00–99). Slots 00–09 are the factory range — slot 00 ships with a user-editable "First Rig" starter preset, and all factory slots can be overwritten. Slots 10–99 are empty user slots by default.

Each preset stores the complete rig state: all 16 effect slot configurations (type, algorithm, parameters, enable state), the signal path and Mix settings, all eight scenes, per-preset stomp assignments, the NAM model references, IR file references, and chain-level enable/bypass. Presets saved on this version need this version: an older build cannot read a preset that uses slots 11 to 16, a split path, scenes or stomps. The Preset Browser (tap the preset name/number in the toolbar) offers a search bar, category filter pills (Factory/User/Custom), and a 4-column visual grid. Presets are stored as JSON.

To share a sound, Ampz exports a rig rather than a bare preset. A preset refers to its NAM model and cabinet IR by filename, so on its own it would arrive on someone else's device with the amp and cab slots empty. An .ampzrig file carries the preset and the models and IRs it uses, so it loads ready to play. Press and hold a preset in the browser and choose Share Rig to send one via AirDrop, Messages, Mail, or Files; Ampz confirms exactly what the file will contain first, since it copies your amp captures into it. Incoming rigs open from the browser's Import Rig button or straight from Files, and land in the first empty slot so nothing is overwritten. If a referenced model or IR did not travel, Ampz names it rather than silently loading a half-empty chain.

Scenes

Every preset has eight scenes. A scene is a snapshot of every pedal's bypass state and parameters plus the Mix block's settings; it does not change the pedal order or the split, which belong to the preset. Switching is gapless: nothing is rebuilt, tails keep ringing, and each bypass flip is crossfaded so it never clicks. Switch from the scene row on the main screen, the large pads in Live Performance, the MIDI Scene 1–8, Scene Up and Scene Down actions, or the plugin's Scene parameter. Edits stick to the active scene, saving the preset saves all eight, and a long-press on a capsule renames it.

Looper

Two independent loop tracks, each with a circular waveform display, Record/Play/Stop/Clear controls, a volume fader, Reverse and Half-Speed toggles, and a mode selector (Normal, One-Shot, Replace, Decay). The looper records post-effects audio — your full processed signal including the NAM amp and every active effect.

Recording flow: tap the circle to start recording, tap again to stop and begin playback, tap while playing to enter overdub (layers new audio on top), tap again to exit overdub. A routing switch selects Parallel (both loops mixed at the output) or Serial (Loop 1 feeds into Loop 2). Optional Quantize snaps record start/stop to Beat, Half Bar, or Bar boundaries at the global BPM.

Drum Loops

A built-in drum loop player for practice accompaniment: 160 professionally recorded AAC loops across 10 genres (Blues, Country, Funk, Jazz, Latin, Metal, Pop, R&B, Reggae, Rock) and 6 time signatures (3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/4, 7/8). Loops are time-stretched in real time to match your global BPM, and can be captured into looper recordings via the Looper Integration toggle.

Tuner

A built-in chromatic tuner with a note-name display, exact frequency readout, and a color-coded cents gauge (green within ±5 cents, amber within ±15, red beyond). A Mute toggle silences output while tuning (auto-disabled when you close the tuner), and the reference pitch for A4 is adjustable from 430–450 Hz (440 Hz default).

Live Performance and Stomps

Live Performance mode is the stage view: an oversized preset readout with foot-sized chevrons, the BPM, eight scene pads, and a 2×5 grid of stomps. A stomp is a virtual footswitch that belongs to Ampz rather than to a slot, so the same switch can mean "the drive pedal" in every preset. By default stomp 1 drives slot 1 through stomp 10 and slot 10; long-press a cell to assign it a name and a target: one pedal, every pedal of a kind (with a lane filter and All or First), or a lane switch (Path A, Path B, Path A/B toggle), for this preset only or for all presets. When the rig is split an amber PATH pad jumps the crossfade between lanes A and B. The screen stays awake while Live mode is open.

MIDI

Ampz organizes MIDI control into five mapping tiers, each with a different identity model:

TierKeyed byUse it for
CC MappingsSlot + parameterA knob/pedal controlling a specific parameter on whichever effect is in that slot
Slot Bypass MappingsSlot indexA footswitch toggling a specific slot position, across all presets
Stomp MappingsStomp 1–10A footswitch driving one of the ten Live stomps, which resolves to the right pedal in every preset
Global Effect MappingsEffect + algorithmA footswitch toggling every instance of an effect (e.g. every Klear) regardless of slot
Action MappingsApp actionGlobal actions — Tap Tempo, Preset Up/Down, Scene 1–8 and Up/Down, Path A/B, looper transport

All mapping types use the same MIDI Learn flow: tap Learn, move the controller, and Ampz captures the CC automatically. Ampz also supports MIDI Program Change (switches presets 0–99) and Bluetooth MIDI pairing for wireless controllers and foot switches.

Tone3000

Tone3000 is a community platform for NAM models and cabinet IRs, integrated directly into Ampz's model and IR browsers. Each capture shows as a rich card with cover art, capture count, contributor handle, and download/favorite stats. Browse/Favorites segmented controls and category chips (Amps, Full Rigs, Pedals, Outboard) narrow the list, and a sort menu offers Trending, Newest, Oldest, or Most Downloaded. A single capture can include multiple file variants (different cabs, mic positions, room takes) — pick exactly the ones you want before downloading; they land in your library with cover thumbnails pre-populated.

The Ampz AUv3 Plugin

Ampz also ships as an Audio Unit v3 (AUv3) effect plugin (type aufx ampz LHNE), embedded inside the app and automatically available to any AUv3 host once Ampz is installed — no separate download. It runs the same DSP engine as the standalone app: the complete 16-slot effects chain with split lanes, the Mix block and scenes, per-slot multi-NAM, host-tempo sync for time-based effects, and a named, host-automatable parameter tree that also exposes the Scene and Mix controls.

Because the plugin runs at the host's sample rate, Ampz brackets the chain with a high-quality resampler so the neural models always run at their native rate — any host rate works. Load it in AUM, Loopy Pro, or GarageBand for iOS by adding an AUv3 effect insert and choosing Ampz from the LNH Enterprises LLC manufacturer group. Force-quit the standalone app first — it claims the audio input and can block the plugin from receiving signal.

Models, IRs, and presets are shared between the app and the plugin via the same on-device library, the plugin can save a rig back into that library, and saving your host project stores the entire rig (chain, signal path, scenes, NAM model references, IR references) so it recalls exactly as you left it. See the dedicated AUv3 amp modeler page for more detail.
